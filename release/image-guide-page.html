How to Write Better AI Image Prompts

A good image prompt is not a list of fashionable adjectives. It is a set of decisions: what the image depicts, what matters most in the frame, how it should be lit, and what the viewer should notice first. At AURIX, you can test those decisions in the Image Studio, which sends your prompt to a real image-generation provider. This guide shows a repeatable way to improve results without pretending that every request will produce the perfect image.

Start with a concrete subject and action

“A futuristic scene” leaves too many choices to the model. Try “A bicycle courier crosses a rain-soaked bridge at dusk, seen from behind.” The second version names a subject, an action, a setting and a camera position. You can now judge whether the result follows the composition you wanted. If not, adjust one part instead of adding a dozen unrelated details.

Specify composition before decoration

Describe whether you want a close-up, wide shot, overhead view or centered portrait. Tell the model where the main subject belongs: “in the left third with open space on the right” is useful when you plan to add a headline later. Composition affects usability as much as beauty. A visually attractive picture may still fail as a website hero if the subject blocks the text area.

Use light, material and mood deliberately

Lighting instructions can be specific: “soft window light from the right,” “overcast afternoon,” or “warm backlight at sunset.” Materials matter too: brushed aluminum, rough paper, wet pavement and translucent glass all create different visual cues. Choose one dominant mood rather than mixing conflicting words such as minimal, chaotic, dark and cheerful in the same short prompt.

Choose the right shape for the destination

A square image works well as a card or profile illustration. A 16:9 landscape is often more useful for a wide article header. A 9:16 portrait suits a vertical mobile layout. These ratios change framing, not just crop. In AURIX you can choose among all three; the image provider limits the total image area, so a wider result is not necessarily higher resolution.

Build a two-pass workflow

  1. Write a simple first prompt with subject, action, scene and composition.
  2. Generate and inspect the real result.
  3. Write down the specific failure: wrong camera angle, poor lighting, too much clutter or missing subject.
  4. Change the prompt to address that failure. Keep successful details intact.
  5. Download a promising result, then check it at the size where you will publish it.

For example, start with: “A small reading nook in a sunlit apartment, wide shot, warm wood and green plants.” If the image becomes cluttered, revise to: “One armchair beside a tall window, a single plant, open space on the left, soft late-afternoon light, minimal interior.” The revision makes the target easier to evaluate.

What the style buttons mean

The Realistic, Cinematic, Anime, Illustration, 3D, Fantasy and Minimalist choices in the studio add descriptive style words to your prompt. They are not seven different models, and results will vary. The underlying anonymous provider can add its own logo, refuse requests or change availability. AURIX shows no fabricated credit balance because the provider does not expose one to anonymous visitors.

Publish responsibly

Avoid putting private data, an identifiable person's likeness without consent, or material you do not have permission to use into a prompt. A generated image may contain unexpected artifacts or misleading details. Review it before publication. The provider's terms and the selected model's license—not this guide—determine your rights, particularly for commercial use. Generated image links may be accessible to anyone who knows the URL, so do not treat them as a private archive.
